OpenAI and Meta warn US judges that deepfakes may outpace detection

The two companies told a federal judicial panel that reliably spotting AI-generated evidence may not be possible.

By the numbers

15 of 931judges who faced a deepfake claim

What happened

OpenAI policy counsel Shira Anderson told the US Judicial Conference's evidence rules committee she did not expect a scientific way to detect every fake. A federal survey in March found 15 of 931 judges had faced a deepfake challenge.

Why it matters

Courts rely on photos and video as evidence, and fakes that cannot be detected threaten that.

Why the hype

  • RiskFake evidence could undermine trust in court proceedings.
  • Big nameThe warning comes from two companies that make image-generating AI.

Background

Federal judicial panels advise on the rules US courts use to admit evidence.

What to watch next

Watch for whether the judiciary proposes new rules for handling digital evidence.
